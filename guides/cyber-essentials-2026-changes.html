Cyber Essentials 2026 changes, in plain English

On 27 April 2026 Cyber Essentials moved to version 3.3 of the requirements and a new question set called Danzell, replacing Willow. Here is what changed for a small business.

1. MFA on cloud services is now a hard rule

If a cloud service offers multi-factor authentication, even as a paid extra, it must be on for every user, not just admins. That includes free accounts used for work. If it is missing, the whole assessment fails.

2. Cloud services can't be left out of scope

Any online service that stores or handles business data is in scope: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Dropbox, your accounts software, your website admin. List them all before you start.

3. The 14-day rule covers more

Critical and high-risk fixes must still be applied within 14 days of release. The rule now includes configuration changes and scripts the vendor recommends, not just software patches, and the questions ask specifically about routers and applications.

4. Scope is stricter

Any device that connects to the internet is in scope. If you leave part of the business out, you must show how it is technically separated, and assessors can challenge scoping that looks designed to make the form easier.

5. A director signs for the whole year

A director or board member declares that the controls stay in place throughout the certificate's 12 months.

Which rules apply to you?

It depends on when you created your assessment account. Accounts created before 27 April 2026 used Willow. Renewals after that date use Danzell, so for most small firms the next renewal is the first one under the new rules.
